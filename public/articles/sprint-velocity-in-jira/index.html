How to calculate sprint velocity in Jira

Velocity is the amount of work a team actually finishes in a sprint, usually measured in story points. Averaged over a few sprints, it tells you how much to plan into the next one. This article shows how to read it in Jira, how to turn it into a planning number, and the mistakes that make it misleading.

What counts#

  • Only finished work. An issue counts toward the sprint in which it was completed. Work started but not done counts for nothing in that sprint.
  • Only estimated work. An issue without story points adds zero, however big it was.
  • The board’s estimation field. Velocity is computed from the one field the board estimates with (its Estimation settings). Points typed into another field are invisible to it.

Reading it in Jira#

On a Scrum board, open Reports and choose the Velocity report. For each recent sprint it shows two bars:

  • Commitment — the estimates of the issues in the sprint when it started;
  • Completed — the estimates of the issues finished by the time it closed.

The gap between them is the useful part. A team that always commits far more than it completes is planning on hope, not on velocity.

Turning it into a planning number#

Take the Completed values of the last few sprints — five is a common choice — and average them:

Sprint Completed
Sprint 20 34
Sprint 21 41
Sprint 22 29
Sprint 23 38
Sprint 24 36
Average 35.6

Plan the next sprint at about that total, then adjust for what you know: holidays, a new teammate, an unusual amount of support work. Use the average, not the best sprint.

Mistakes that distort velocity#

  • Changing estimates after the work is done. Re-pointing a finished issue rewrites history, and the average stops describing the team.
  • Comparing teams. Points are relative to the team that assigned them; one team’s 40 is not another’s.
  • Counting half-done work. Splitting an unfinished issue and crediting the “done half” inflates the number. Carry the issue over and let it count when it is finished.
  • Too few sprints. One or two sprints are noise. Wait for four or five before trusting the average.

Planning against velocity in one table#

Scope Planner shows the sprint total next to a target velocity while you estimate, so you see the sprint overflow before you start it, not halfway through.

  • The totals bar shows the sprint’s story points and, when a target velocity is set, a capacity indicator that highlights when the draft total goes over it.
  • With Pro, ✨ Auto-calculate fills the target from the average of the board’s last five completed sprints — no number to maintain by hand.
  • Also with Pro, the Workload tab shows each assignee’s points and flags anyone above 1.5× the team average, so you can rebalance before the sprint starts.

The estimation table, drafts and totals are free; target velocity, workload and publishing to Jira are Pro — see Free and Pro and Preferences in the user guide.
